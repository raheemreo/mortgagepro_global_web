US Mortgage Calculator: Monthly Payments, Rates and the Full Budget

Calculate US mortgage principal and interest, compare 15- and 30-year examples, and separate a dated rate benchmark from your actual lender quote.

Publisher: REO TechnologiesOriginally published: 2026-07-03Updated: October 25, 2026

A house can look affordable in a search result and feel expensive once the whole monthly bill arrives. Start with principal and interest, but leave room for property taxes, homeowners insurance and any mortgage insurance. The number a basic mortgage calculator gives you is only the first part of that budget.

This guide uses a $412,500 home and a $41,250 down payment. The resulting loan is $371,250. You can change those assumptions below; the calculator estimates a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan rather than an approval or a lender offer.

Which interest rate should you enter?

Use the note rate on a lender's quote when you have one. For context, Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey reported 7.32% for a 30-year fixed mortgage and 6.68% for a 15-year fixed mortgage on October 22, 2026, the latest weekly observation checked for this update. Those are national benchmarks, not a promise of your rate. Credit, loan features, discount points and the date of a quote can change your offer.

The examples below keep that observation date attached to the rates. They will not become a new market quote when the next survey is released. The editable rate field starts at 7.32%; replace it with your own quote before making a budget.

Follow one purchase through the calculation

InputExample assumption
Purchase price$412,500
Down payment$41,250, or 10%
Starting principal$371,250
Fixed annual note rate7.32%
Repayment term30 years / 360 monthly payments
Monthly principal and interest$2,550.23

The payment formula is M = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n). Here P is principal, n is the number of monthly payments, and r is the monthly rate expressed as a decimal: 7.32 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.0061. At a zero interest rate, the payment is simply P ÷ n.

A fixed-rate loan keeps scheduled principal and interest steady, assuming you follow its terms. Your total housing payment can still increase when taxes, insurance or other charges change. For the separate budget lines, use the PITI guide.

Compare the term as well as the monthly payment

Using the same $371,250 principal, the dated 15-year benchmark of 6.68% produces a monthly P&I payment of $3,270.84, compared with $2,550.23 over 30 years at 7.32%. With no extra payments, modeled total interest is about $217,500.35 versus $546,832.77. This comparison changes both the rate and the term; it does not isolate the effect of term alone.

That is a $720.61 larger monthly commitment in the shorter example. The practical question is whether that commitment leaves enough cash for repairs, emergencies and other priorities. A lower lifetime interest bill does not make every higher monthly payment comfortable.

A small rate change is worth checking

Holding principal and the 30-year term fixed, a hypothetical 6.82% rate would reduce P&I to $2,425.22, about $125.01 less each month. This is a sensitivity example, not an available offer. Buying a lower rate with points or refinancing an existing loan introduces costs that this comparison does not include.

When comparing offers, look at the interest rate, APR, points, lender credits, cash to close and expected holding period together. The CFPB explanation of rate versus APR explains why they differ. APR adds certain borrowing costs; it does not replace comparing similar loans on their Loan Estimates.

Before you rely on the result

  • Add local taxes, an insurance quote, any PMI, HOA dues and maintenance. Avoid treating a national cost estimate as a local bill.
  • Use the amount actually financed. Financed program fees can make it larger than purchase price minus down payment.
  • Keep extra principal payments separate from the scheduled payment. The amortization guide shows the effect of an extra $200 each month.
  • An adjustable-rate loan needs its reset terms and caps modeled separately. A single fixed-rate result does not describe its future payments.

Questions readers often ask

Why does my Loan Estimate show a different payment?

Check principal, note rate, term and the breakdown of mortgage insurance and escrow. Then compare its Estimated Total Monthly Payment and Estimated Taxes, Insurance & Assessments. The CFPB Loan Estimate explainer shows where to find those figures, including costs paid outside escrow.

Does this calculator decide whether I qualify?

No. It does not review credit, documented income, assets, property eligibility or lender underwriting. Use it to prepare questions for a lender, not as a preapproval.

Does a larger down payment always get the lowest rate?

No. It reduces the principal, but the rate depends on the complete loan profile. Compare written offers and keep enough cash for closing costs and a reserve.
