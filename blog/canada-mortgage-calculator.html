Canada Mortgage Calculator: Payments, Compounding and Renewal

Estimate Canadian fixed-rate mortgage payments with semiannual compounding and distinguish the current contract term from the amortization period.

Publisher: REO TechnologiesOriginally published: 2026-04-01Updated: October 25, 2026

A five-year mortgage term and a 25-year amortization answer different questions. The term tells you when the current agreement ends; amortization describes the repayment schedule. A payment estimate needs both ideas, even though the monthly formula uses the amortization.

For a CAD 650,000 purchase with CAD 130,000 down, principal is CAD 520,000. At an assumed 3.99% nominal rate compounded semiannually over 25 years, modeled monthly principal and interest are CAD 2,732.49. The rate is a scenario input, not a current Bank of Canada retail offer.

Try the assumptions

Amounts are CAD. Enter your current figures, then press Calculate estimate. The tool does not establish approval.

Use the rate convention in the contract

For this fixed-rate convention, the monthly decimal rate is (1 + annual percentage / 200)^(1/6) − 1. At the same nominal annual quote this monthly rate is slightly lower than annual percentage / 1200, not higher. The payment formula then spreads principal over 300 payments.

Do not assume every Canadian variable-rate or other product uses that convention. Check the mortgage disclosure. The FCAC mortgage calculator is a useful independent comparison, and FCAC's mortgage information explains the product choices.

Keep the monthly number in its proper scope

The example excludes property tax, heating, condo fees, insurance and closing costs. If a default-insurance premium is financed, enter the increased principal rather than price minus down payment alone. Our CMHC calculator separates that calculation.

A constant 3.99% across the full 25 years is only a modeling assumption. The example does not predict renewal rates. Use the renewal planner with the balance remaining at the end of the current term and the remaining amortization.

Before comparing lenders

Ask whether the quote includes points or fees, which payment frequency it assumes, what prepayments are permitted, and what happens when the term expires. A lower payment from extending amortization is not the same as a lower cost on an unchanged repayment timeline.

Does this establish eligibility?

No. The stress-test guide covers a separate qualifying calculation. Credit, documented income, property and insurer requirements are not checked here.

Can I enter a zero rate?

Yes. Principal is divided by the payment count. Blank or invalid fields clear the result rather than leaving an earlier answer on screen.
