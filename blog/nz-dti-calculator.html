New Zealand DTI: A Debt Ratio, Not a Personal Borrowing Entitlement

Calculate New Zealand debt-to-income and distinguish RBNZ's six- and seven-times lender speed limits from a guaranteed individual mortgage maximum.

Publisher contact: Abdul RaheemPublisher: REO TechnologiesUpdated: October 25, 2026

Multiplying income by six and subtracting a car loan is a useful sensitivity exercise, but it is not a bank's complete borrowing offer. The regulatory framework restricts shares of high-DTI lending, while serviceability, deposit, property and credit can set different constraints for an individual.

Keep debt balances and payments separate

For a simple owner-occupier illustration, NZD 900,000 debt divided by NZD 150,000 annual qualifying income equals 6.00 times. Adding NZD 35,000 assessed other debt raises the ratio to 6.23 times. A monthly car payment should not be added to a mortgage balance; use the appropriate assessed balance or limit under the lender's method.

If you choose six times income as a planning threshold, subtracting a NZD 35,000 debt from NZD 900,000 leaves NZD 865,000. That only describes the debt room at the selected ratio. It does not say the bank can or will approve an NZD 865,000 mortgage.

Understand the actual speed limits

RBNZ's DTI explainer permits up to 20% of owner-occupier lending above six times income and up to 20% of investor lending above seven times. These are bank lending-flow limits, not personal hard caps. In this framework greater than six is different from Australia's greater than or equal to six high-DTI definition.

Exemptions and measurement rules have conditions. Certain construction, refinancing or supported-loan arrangements can receive different treatment, but a borrower should ask the bank to confirm the specific case rather than ticking a universal exemption.

Serviceability remains important

A bank can assess income differently from total salary, shade rent, include credit limits or use a stressed payment. A ratio below the policy threshold does not make the actual payment sustainable. Use a household cash budget as well as the lender's qualification method.

Sorted's borrowing guide explains why repayment changes and other expenses matter. The calculator does not see the bank's available high-DTI headroom or create a right to use it.

Does repaying another debt help?

It can improve the balance ratio and monthly cash flow, but may use money needed for the deposit and reserve. Consider both sides with the lender before moving funds.
