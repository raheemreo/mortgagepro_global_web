CMHC Premium Calculator: Base Loan, Surcharge and Cash Tax

Estimate a standard Canadian high-ratio purchase premium with verified CMHC tiers, a 30-year surcharge and explicit eligibility limits.

Publisher contact: Abdul RaheemPublisher: REO TechnologiesUpdated: October 25, 2026

Mortgage default insurance protects the lender, not the buyer's ability to keep making payments. A premium passed to you can be paid at closing or added to principal. Financing it means interest is charged on a larger balance.

This calculator covers a traditional-down-payment, owner-occupied one- or two-unit purchase. It does not price every insurer, property type, non-traditional deposit or refinance.

Try the assumptions

Amounts are CAD. Enter your current figures, then press Calculate estimate. The tool does not establish approval.

Apply the tier to the base loan

For the supported high-ratio scenario, CMHC's published purchase tiers are 2.80% above 80% through 85% LTV, 3.10% above 85% through 90%, and 4.00% above 90% through 95%. Amortization beyond 25 years adds 0.20 percentage points, where the buyer qualifies for that longer insured amortization. See the CMHC premium schedule.

The rate is applied before adding the premium, not recursively to the financed total. At 20% down the widget sets this borrower-paid high-ratio charge to zero; low-ratio lender insurance is a different question.

Check eligibility before treating the number as a quote

CMHC Purchase specifies a purchase/lending value below CAD 1,500,000. The standard minimum equity rule for the modeled property is 5% of the first CAD 500,000 and 10% of the remainder. At CAD 1,500,000 the high-ratio insured scenario is not available here.

Thirty-year insured amortization requires the applicable first-time-buyer or new-build eligibility, confirmed through CMHC Home Start. Selecting a field does not establish that eligibility.

Separate cash tax from financed premium

The premium-tax field is an explicit assumption: use the rate applicable to your transaction, or zero where none applies. Provincial tax on the premium cannot simply be financed with it. The model does not determine the province's tax treatment, portability credits, insurer refunds or energy-efficiency incentives.

Do all insurers charge the same price?

Do not assume so. Ask the lender to identify the insurer, premium, eligibility and any credit in writing. Use the insurance checklist to prepare that conversation.
