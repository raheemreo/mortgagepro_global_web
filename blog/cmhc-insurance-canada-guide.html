Canadian Mortgage Insurance: A Checklist Before You Commit

Understand what mortgage default insurance covers, which purchase costs stay in cash, and what to confirm with the lender before financing a premium.

Publisher: REO TechnologiesOriginally published: 2026-04-14Updated: October 25, 2026

A premium estimate is useful only if you know whose insurance it is and what transaction it applies to. Mortgage default insurance protects the lender's loss exposure; it is not life, disability, unemployment or homeowners insurance for you.

This guide is a purchase checklist. For numerical tiers and a working estimator, use the separate CMHC premium calculator.

Confirm the property and borrowing category

The CMHC Purchase requirements distinguish property use and unit count, down-payment sources, lending value and amortization. The standard owner-occupied one- or two-unit model is not a universal rule for every rental or refinance.

At a CAD 600,000 price, the standard tiered minimum down payment is CAD 35,000. The base loan is CAD 565,000. With a 4.00% premium assumption for a standard 25-year high-ratio scenario, premium is CAD 22,600; if entirely financed, principal becomes CAD 587,600. Other eligibility requirements still apply.

Ask five questions in writing

  • Which insurer and program are being used, and what conditions remain before insurance approval?
  • What lending value and base-loan LTV determined the tier?
  • Is the amortization 25 or 30 years, and is any surcharge included?
  • How much premium is financed, and what premium tax or other charges must be paid in cash?
  • Does an existing insured loan qualify for a portability credit or other program benefit?

CMHC is not the only insurer. Do not assume every provider has identical prices or that a premium is always refunded when you later reach 20% equity. The CMHC published schedule and credits identify the applicable program rather than a universal rule for competitors.

Keep cash to close separate

A down payment is not the complete closing budget. Legal costs, land-transfer taxes, adjustments, inspections, initial tax/insurance needs and any tax on a financed premium can still require cash. Ask the lender and conveyancer for an itemized statement and preserve a contingency reserve.

Does 20% down mean no insurance can exist?

It usually removes the need for this borrower-paid high-ratio premium in a standard purchase. Lenders may arrange low-ratio insurance for their own purposes. Ask what you are actually being charged rather than using 'no insurance' as a blanket description.
