# New Zealand First-Home Support: Separate the Loan, Withdrawal and Closed Grant

**Meta description:** Check New Zealand First Home Loan and KiwiSaver withdrawal conditions without budgeting for the discontinued First Home Grant or obsolete price-cap assumptions.

The First Home Grant and the First Home Loan are different products. A KiwiSaver withdrawal is different again: it uses eligible savings already held for you. Mixing the names can put money into a deposit plan that will not actually be available.

## Do not budget for a new First Home Grant

Kāinga Ora ceased accepting new First Home Grant applications on **May 22, 2024**. Its [home-ownership information](https://kaingaora.govt.nz/home-ownership/) identifies current routes. Leave historical grant amounts out of a new application budget. Check the current official options before making an offer.

## Check the First Home Loan with a participating lender

A [First Home Loan](https://kaingaora.govt.nz/home-ownership/first-home-loan/) can allow an eligible buyer to use a 5% deposit. It is still a mortgage to repay, with lender credit and affordability requirements—not a grant or automatic approval.

Use the current household-income and eligibility criteria for your applicant group. Do not rely on a single rough income figure or the [old regional First Home Loan price-cap framework](https://kaingaora.govt.nz/news/budget-2022-changes-announced-for-first-home-products/). Ask about property requirements, any insurance premium or fee, and how the bank confirms applicable regulatory exemptions. An exemption does not remove the repayment obligation or lender assessment.

## KiwiSaver is an eligible withdrawal, not unrestricted cash

[IRD's first-home guidance](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/getting-my-kiwisaver-funds-early/getting-my-kiwisaver-for-my-first-home) requires at least three years of membership and leaves a required balance. Some transferred funds cannot be withdrawn. Obtain the provider's confirmation and processing instructions before making a purchase commitment.

For an NZD 650,000 price, a 5% deposit is **NZD 32,500**. That calculation does not establish loan eligibility. If a provider confirms NZD 61,000 for the purchase, do not assume the excess is automatically paid to your personal account for unrelated costs. The provider and solicitor control the permitted release and transaction process.

## Keep a completion reserve

Ask for legal, valuation, inspection, moving, rates, insurance and any financed-fee details. A deposit meeting a scheme percentage does not prove that the full cash requirement is covered.

### Can a former owner ever use a first-home withdrawal?

There can be additional criteria and a separate determination. Use the provider and Kāinga Ora process rather than treating all prior owners as automatically eligible or automatically excluded.

### Which information should I get first?

Start with lender eligibility and a provider withdrawal estimate, then have the solicitor confirm deadlines and permitted use. The [KiwiSaver guide](/blog/nz-kiwisaver-calculator) separates the numerical illustration from approval.
