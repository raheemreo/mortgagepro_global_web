# Canada Renewal Planner: Recalculate the Balance and Remaining Term

**Meta description:** Model a Canadian renewal payment from the amortized remaining balance and distinguish a straight switch from new borrowing.

A renewal comparison should start with what you still owe, not the original mortgage amount. Keeping the original amortization when five years have already passed can make a new payment look artificially small.

The planner below amortizes the original loan for the entered elapsed years, then calculates a new payment over the remaining amortization. Old and new rates are editable assumptions, not forecasts or current lender offers.

## Follow the balance into renewal

Use a CAD 500,000 starting loan, an assumed 1.89% rate and 25-year original amortization. After 60 scheduled payments at that rate, use the calculated balance shown above. A new 3.99% scenario then uses the remaining 20 years.

Compare both the monthly difference and total interest over a consistent horizon. Extending the remaining amortization may lower the bill while increasing financing cost. The [FCAC renewal guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/renew-mortgage.html) recommends reviewing the offer and the cost of extending repayment.

## A straight-switch exception is not a universal waiver

[OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages) does not expect its prescribed MQR on qualifying uninsured straight switches between federally regulated lenders at renewal without increased balance or amortization. That is narrower than saying every renewal or lender switch skips every affordability assessment.

Insured transfers, provincial lenders, additional borrowing, property changes and extensions can involve different rules and lender checks. Ask which exception actually applies before relying on it. A new lender can still review credit, income and the property.

## Shop with the documents in front of you

Gather the renewal date, current balance, remaining amortization, current offer, transfer/discharge costs, insurance status and any desired changes. Compare a same-lender offer with alternatives on the same repayment timeline and ask whether fees erase the rate difference.

Starting early can help, but a rate-hold window and automatic-renewal terms are lender-specific. Read the notice rather than assuming every lender offers the same deadline or fallback rate.

### Does this predict payment increases for all 2026 renewals?

No. A market forecast for a cohort is not your loan outcome. The tool calculates only the entered path and does not forecast future interest rates, income or lender approval.

### What if I am already near renewal?

Use the lender's actual outstanding balance and remaining amortization in its quote. This original-loan simulation cannot reproduce missed payments, past extra principal or variable-rate history that you have not entered.
