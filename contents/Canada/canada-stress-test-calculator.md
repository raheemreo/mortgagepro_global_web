# Canada Mortgage Stress Test: Contract Rate Versus Qualifying Rate

**Meta description:** Compare Canadian contract and qualifying payments using the standard 5.25% floor and two-point buffer, with clearly scoped renewal exceptions.

Your contractual payment and a qualifying payment serve different purposes. The first is calculated under the agreed loan; the second helps a lender assess repayment capacity under a higher-rate scenario. Neither replaces credit or income verification.

For the uninsured federally regulated framework, [OSFI's current MQR](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages) is the greater of **5.25% and the contract rate plus two percentage points**. These settings were checked on October 25, 2026.

## Check both branches of the formula

| Contract-rate assumption | Standard modeled qualifying rate |
|---|---|
| 3.00% | 5.25% |
| 3.25% | 5.25% |
| 3.99% | 5.99% |
| 5.00% | 7.00% |

On CAD 520,000 over 25 years, using semiannual compounding, the 3.99% scenario gives **CAD 2,732.49 monthly**. At 5.99% the modeled payment is **CAD 3,323.90**, a CAD 591.42 difference before other housing costs. The lender does not automatically collect that higher assessment amount.

## Put the result into the right assessment

A lender can include taxes, heating and other housing charges, other debts, documented qualifying income and financed insurance premiums. The [GDS/TDS guide](/blog/canada-gds-tds-calculator) keeps those inputs separate. A stress-tested payment alone is not the complete application.

Insured mortgages have their own government/insurer criteria; B-20 should not be named as the direct rule for every loan. Provincial credit unions and other lenders can operate under different requirements and their own assessment policies. 'Not subject to this OSFI rule' does not mean 'no affordability test.'

## Renewal and transfer need a scope check

Qualifying uninsured straight switches between federally regulated lenders, with no increase in balance or amortization, have an exception to OSFI's prescribed MQR. Do not turn that into a blanket promise for cash-out refinancing, an extension or every insured transfer. The lender should identify the applicable treatment for your case.

### Is the qualifying rate a prediction?

No. It is a regulatory assessment parameter, not a forecast, retail offer or cap on the rate you might encounter later. A household should still plan for changing expenses and income.

### Does a longer amortization always solve qualification?

It can lower the mathematical payment but requires product eligibility and increases the time principal remains outstanding. It does not waive other underwriting requirements.
