# SDLT Completion Checklist: Confirm the Facts Before Filing

**Meta description:** Prepare the transaction details needed for an England or Northern Ireland SDLT return, including relief, residence and potential refund questions.

A correctly calculated tax band can still give the wrong bill if the buyer category or transaction facts are wrong. This checklist complements the [SDLT calculator](/blog/uk-stamp-duty-calculator); it focuses on what to confirm with the conveyancer rather than repeating another table of bands.

## Start with the jurisdiction and transaction

SDLT covers England and Northern Ireland. A Scottish or Welsh property has a different tax system. Confirm the effective transaction date, consideration, whether the property is residential, whether other purchases are linked, and whether lease rent needs a separate calculation.

The [HMRC SDLT overview](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax) links the relevant rates and filing guidance. Ordinary completion is a common effective date, but substantial performance can change the timing, so 'the day you get the keys' is not a complete legal definition.

## Establish the purchaser facts

- List every purchaser and any company, trust or partnership involvement.
- Disclose property interests already held, including interests outside the UK, and whether a main residence is being replaced.
- Ask whether each buyer meets all first-time-relief conditions; being a first-time mortgage borrower is not the same thing.
- Check the SDLT-specific residence test rather than assuming passport or current address settles it.
- Confirm any special relief, exemption or higher-rate treatment with the person preparing the return.

## Make the cash budget explicit

On a supported standard £350,000 individual purchase, the banded example gives **£7,500**. A qualifying first-time scenario at the same price gives **£2,500**. These are illustration categories, not confirmation of your relief. Tax usually needs a separate cash budget rather than being mistaken for the deposit.

Ask for the completion statement early enough to check the tax, legal charges, deposits already paid and other adjustments. Keep copies of the calculation and filed return.

## Filing and refunds have different clocks

A standard SDLT return and payment are generally due within **14 days of the effective date**, subject to the applicable rules. A conveyancer handling the process does not remove the need to check it was completed.

Where replacing a main residence qualifies for repayment of additional-property tax after a later sale, [HMRC's higher-rate guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/stamp-duty-land-tax-buying-an-additional-residential-property) provides the sale and claim conditions. The claim deadline can run from the later of the relevant sale or SDLT return filing date; do not rely on an incomplete '12 months from sale' rule or assume a refund is automatic.

### Does this cover every relief?

No. Bring unusual facts to the conveyancer rather than forcing them into a standard online buyer-type selection. Check the rules at the transaction date, not only when you first viewed the property.
