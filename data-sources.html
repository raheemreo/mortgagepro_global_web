Financial Data Sources

Find the primary sources behind MortgagePro Global's policy references and published observations, with dates and limits on how to use them.

Document revision 2026-10-26 · Publisher: REO Technologies. Source observations have their own dates.

A central bank's policy rate, a published mortgage survey and a personal lender quote are different kinds of information. The site labels references and observations so you can see what a number represents before using it.

For a policy decision, the effective date is when the recorded rate took effect. For a daily or weekly series, the date is the source's observation date; it is not necessarily the date of a rate change. The source-checked date records retrieval or review, not a new market observation. An unchanged policy rate can have an older effective date; a fresh page-load timestamp does not make an old figure newly verified. Policy references are flagged for review after their recorded review date.

Published mortgage and banking observations

US mortgage survey observations come from Freddie Mac PMMS, accessed through FRED. The survey is a dated benchmark, not a live quote for each borrower. Other FRED series, such as prime or an effective policy rate, measure different instruments.

Canadian observations use the Bank of Canada Valet API. A conventional mortgage benchmark or chartered-bank prime series is not automatically the best retail offer for a particular applicant.

Automated retrieval preserves the observation date. If a source fails, we retain the actual verification date and mark old observations accordingly. Unverified historical lender and market figures are labeled historical rather than represented as current quotes.

Rules, taxes and insurance

Use CFPB for US consumer explanations, CMHC and OSFI for the applicable Canadian frameworks, and HMRC for England/Northern Ireland SDLT.

Australian transfer-duty assessments come from the relevant state or territory revenue office. APRA explains prudential lending settings. RBNZ explains New Zealand's lending-flow restrictions, while IRD and Kāinga Ora provide tax, KiwiSaver and housing-support information.

Indian tax treatment should be checked for the relevant tax year through the Income Tax Department. A broad European repayment example cannot replace a country's tax assessment or a notary's quote.

Calculator defaults and updates

Editable loan rates, prices and costs are scenario inputs unless explicitly identified as dated source observations. They are not silently set equal to policy rates. Read the methodology and the individual tool's limitations, and obtain a lender quote before a decision.

Source links can change. Report a broken link or a discrepancy through the corrections policy. Source selection and automated checks do not constitute a guarantee that all information remains current at every moment.
