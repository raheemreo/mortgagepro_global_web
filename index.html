Mortgage Terminology

Mortgage Glossary - Key Terms Explained

New to mortgages? Click any term to learn what it means in plain English.

Amortization is the process of paying off your loan through scheduled, regular payments over time. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal. A 30-year mortgage has 360 monthly payments before it's fully paid off.
PITI means principal, interest, property taxes and homeowners insurance in US usage. Mortgage insurance, HOA and other ownership costs should be identified separately. Escrow arrangements vary by lender and loan.
PMI may apply to a conventional low-deposit loan and protects the lender. Pricing, cancellation and automatic termination have conditions. Reaching 20% equity through appreciation does not automatically cancel a charge.
LTV compares assessed mortgage debt with the property value used for lending. USD 360,000 on a USD 450,000 property is 80% LTV. Valuation, insurance and lender eligibility need separate checks.
EMI is the fixed monthly amount you pay toward your home loan in India. It's calculated using the formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ - 1), where P is principal, r is monthly interest rate, and n is number of months.
Mortgage default insurance protects the lender, not the borrower. CMHC premiums depend on the loan-to-value ratio, product and amortization; a 4% ceiling does not cover every case. The premium may be added to the loan, while applicable provincial premium tax must be paid separately. Confirm eligibility and the exact cost with your lender. See the official CMHC premium schedule (source checked October 30, 2026).

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

The most common mortgage questions - answered clearly and concisely.

  • A calculator estimates repayment from the principal, rate, convention and payment period you enter. Other ownership costs are included only where explicitly shown. It does not assess credit, income or lender approval.

  • The models use stated assumptions and dated source information. Daily interest, payment dates, fees, rate changes and product terms can make an actual lender schedule differ. Confirm figures against the written offer rather than treating an estimate as an exact bill.

  • Begin with a household budget, existing commitments and a closing-cost reserve. Lenders apply different qualifying-income, expense, credit and property rules. A single debt-to-income percentage does not establish a universal maximum or an approval.

  • Principal and interest depend on financed balance, rate and repayment time. Property tax, homeowners insurance, any mortgage insurance, association charges and other local costs are separate unless the tool explicitly adds them.

  • Choose the property location and check the model assumptions. For example, a Canadian fixed-rate illustration can use a semiannual quote convention, while other products may calculate daily interest. A regional page does not automatically assess every local tax or eligibility category.
